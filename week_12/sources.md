# Week 12 Sources and Research Anchors

Research checked on April 28, 2026.

## Course anchors
- `CST_2412.pdf`
  The syllabus lists Week 11-12 as **Data Analytics for Security**, Week 13 as **Data Poisoning and Model Evasion**, Week 14 as **Final Project Presentations**, and Week 15 as **Final Project Due and Wrap-Up**.

- Pfleeger, C. P., Pfleeger, S. L., & Margulies, J. (2015). *Security in Computing* (5th ed.). Pearson.
  Useful textbook connections include audit logs, intrusion detection, database security, data mining, privacy risk, and privacy-preserving data mining.

## External research anchors
- NIST SP 800-92 Rev. 1 Initial Public Draft, **Cybersecurity Log Management Planning Guide**  
  https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/92/r1/ipd  
  Used to support the idea that logs are generated, stored, analyzed, and disposed of for security operations, incident investigation, operational troubleshooting, and retention needs.

- NIST SP 800-181, **NICE Cybersecurity Workforce Framework**  
  https://www.nist.gov/document/nistsp800-181pdf  
  Used to connect beginner analytics projects to cybersecurity work roles, especially data analysis work that examines multiple sources to provide security and privacy insight.

- NIST Privacy Framework  
  https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework  
  Used to frame privacy as enterprise risk management and to remind students that security projects should include privacy thinking.

- NIST Privacy Risk Assessment  
  https://www.nist.gov/itl/applied-cybersecurity/privacy-engineering/collaboration-space/privacy-risk-assessment  
  Used to support the final project requirement that students discuss privacy risks arising from data processing.

- OWASP Developer Guide: OWASP Top 10  
  https://devguide.owasp.org/en/02-foundations/05-top-ten/  
  Used as a beginner-friendly source for application security topics, especially logging and monitoring failures, broken access control, cryptographic failures, injection, authentication failures, and insecure design.

## Design choice
The final project options are intentionally small.
Beginner projects work best when students can:
- ask one focused question
- use safe fictional or public data
- make a small number of findings
- connect those findings to course concepts
- give a realistic recommendation

This is why Week 12 gives students two equal paths:
- a coding option for students who want to build a small notebook, script, or app
- a research essay option for students who want to analyze a case, technology, or policy issue
