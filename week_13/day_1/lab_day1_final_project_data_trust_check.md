## Purpose
Today you will use the idea of **data poisoning** to improve your final project.

Data poisoning means that data can be shaped, corrupted, mislabeled, manipulated, or made misleading before anyone analyzes it. You do not need to build or attack a model today. Your job is simpler:

**Check whether the evidence for your final project is trustworthy enough to use.**

## Part 3: Dataset or Source Trust Check
Choose your most important evidence item.

Answer in short sentences.

1. What is the evidence item?
2. Who created or published it?

## Part 4: Data Poisoning Thinking
Answer each question in **2-3 sentences**.

1. If this evidence is a dataset, what could make the data inaccurate, incomplete, mislabeled, or biased?
2. If this evidence is a written source, what could make the source incomplete, outdated, one-sided, or misleading?
3. What would happen to your final project argument if this evidence were wrong?
4. What is one way you can reduce that risk?

Examples of risk-reduction steps:
- compare two sources
- read the dataset documentation
- check column definitions
- explain missing values
- avoid broad claims
- use one source as an example, not as total proof
- include a limitation paragraph
