# Week 10: Privacy, Law, and Ethics Through Chun, Benjamin, and Forensic Architecture

## Week focus
Week 10 now has two class meetings that fit together.

Day 1 focuses on theory and critique:
- Ruha Benjamin on technology, power, and collective imagination
- Wendy Hui Kyong Chun on correlation, prediction, repetition, and data categories

Day 2 focuses on method and evidence:
- Forensic Architecture as a model for investigating human rights cases
- spatial reconstruction as a way to test claims about what happened
- technical workflows that turn fragmented media into structured evidence

## Core thread for the week
- move from critique of data systems to investigation of real events
- ask how technical methods can support accountability rather than just optimization
- connect data, models, media, testimony, and ethics
- help students write about both ideas and method

## Recommended class flow

### Day 1
1. Lecture and discussion deck on Benjamin and Chun
2. Guided comparison using the discussion guide
3. In-class writing response on technology, data, and social power

### Day 2
1. Quick recap (5-10 min): remind students that Day 1 focused on critique, while Day 2 focuses on method
2. Lecture deck (20-25 min): teach Forensic Architecture's approach, vocabulary, and workflow
3. Video and discussion (25-35 min): use the Blender Conference lecture and pause to clarify the method
4. In-class writing (20-25 min): students explain and evaluate the method-centered approach
5. Debrief (5-10 min): discuss when computational reconstruction helps and where ethical limits remain

## Instructor note
The strongest transition from Day 1 to Day 2 is this:
- Day 1 asks how technology and data can reproduce power.
- Day 2 asks how technical tools can also be used to challenge official narratives and document harm.

That contrast helps students see that the ethical question is not only whether technology exists, but how it is built, used, and governed.
