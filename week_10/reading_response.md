# In-Class Writing Assignment: Analyzing Claims About Technology, Data, and Society

## Videos
- Ruha Benjamin, TED Talk: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QO3nY_u6hos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QO3nY_u6hos)
- Wendy Hui Kyong Chun, *Discriminating Data*: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMTGBecxfk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMTGBecxfk)

## Context
Today we watched Ruha Benjamin's TED Talk and Wendy Hui Kyong Chun's talk *Discriminating Data.* Both speakers make arguments about the relationship between technology, data, and social power. This assignment asks you to engage those arguments carefully and critically.

## Objective
Write a short response that explains and evaluates ideas from both talks in relation to a concrete technological system.

## Instructions
Write a response of **350 to 500 words** during class.

Your response should do all of the following:

1. **Summarize one key claim from Ruha Benjamin**  
   Identify one argument, concept, or example from the TED Talk and explain it accurately and fairly.

2. **Summarize one key claim from Wendy Chun**  
   Identify one argument, concept, or example from *Discriminating Data* and explain it accurately and fairly.

3. **Apply those ideas to a real technology**  
   Choose one technology, platform, or system such as:
   - facial recognition
   - recommendation algorithms
   - predictive policing
   - school surveillance software
   - hiring tools
   - credit scoring
   - social media feeds
   - spam filters
   - campus security systems
   - AI chatbots

4. **Evaluate the speakers’ arguments**  
   Consider whether their claims help explain something important about your chosen technology. You may agree, disagree, or partly agree, but your response should make a reasoned argument.

5. **Reflect on STEM practice**  
   End by answering this question:  
   **What kinds of ethical or technical responsibilities should STEM students consider when building, studying, or deploying such systems?**

## Guiding Questions
You do not need to answer all of these, but they may help:
- What assumptions do the speakers make about technology and society?
- Are their arguments convincing in the case you chose? Why or why not?
- What does your example suggest about the relationship between technical performance and ethical consequences?
- Are there limits to either speaker’s perspective?
- What forms of evidence would you want in order to fully assess these claims?
- What kinds of knowledge, beyond coding or statistics, might be useful when evaluating real-world systems?

## Requirements
- 350 to 500 words
- Refer specifically to **both** talks
- Discuss **one concrete technology**
- Write in full sentences
- Make a clear argument, not just a summary
- You are **not** required to agree with either speaker

## Submission
Submit at the end of class.

## Evaluation Criteria
Your response will be evaluated on:
- clarity of writing
- accurate representation of both talks
- quality of analysis
- strength of reasoning
- thoughtful engagement with a real technological example

## Optional Challenge
If you finish early, add one paragraph identifying a point where the two speakers overlap or differ most sharply, and explain why that difference matters.
