# Day 2 In-Class Writing Assignment: Forensic Architecture and Spatial Analysis as Evidence

## Video
- Forensic Architecture, Blender Conference 2022: [Spatial Analysis for Human Rights Cases](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RWPdSJ1-Dvs)

## Context
Today we are looking at Forensic Architecture as a method-centered approach to investigating human rights cases.
Instead of treating architecture as only buildings or design, this approach treats space, viewpoint, sequence, movement, and media evidence as part of an investigative process.
The video shows how photos, phone videos, satellite imagery, maps, and witness accounts can be aligned in time and space to test what happened.

## Objective
Write a short response that explains how Forensic Architecture turns fragmented media and testimony into structured spatial evidence, and evaluate what makes that approach powerful and difficult.

## Instructions
Write a response of **350 to 500 words** during class.

Your response should do all of the following:

1. **Explain the central method**
   Describe Forensic Architecture's main idea in your own words.
   Your response should explain how space, time, viewpoint, and reconstruction become part of an evidentiary process.

2. **Describe at least two techniques**
   Choose at least **two** techniques from the lecture, such as:
   - 3D modelling
   - camera matching
   - photogrammetry
   - geolocation
   - remote sensing
   - synchronization
   - situated testimony

   Explain what each technique helps investigators do.

3. **Explain why combining sources matters**
   Show how witness accounts, visual media, maps, or satellite images become stronger when they are aligned together rather than used separately.

4. **Evaluate the method**
   Identify:
   - **one strength** of this approach
   - **one limitation, risk, or challenge**

   You may discuss accuracy, access to evidence, bias, ethics, trauma, interpretation, reproducibility, or the politics of official narratives.

5. **Reflect on STEM responsibility**
   End by answering this question:
   **What should technical students keep in mind when using computational tools to investigate, represent, or communicate human harm?**

## Guiding Questions
You do not need to answer all of these directly, but they may help:
- What makes this approach different from a normal documentary or opinion piece?
- Why does Forensic Architecture build 3D environments instead of only collecting screenshots?
- How can camera position, line of sight, shadows, or sound become evidence?
- Why might model-assisted testimony be useful in some investigations?
- What ethical responsibilities arise when reconstructing violence, destruction, or witness memory?
- What would make this process convincing to a public audience, court, or journalist?

## Requirements
- 350 to 500 words
- refer specifically to the video
- explain the method, not just the topic
- discuss at least two techniques
- identify one strength and one limitation
- write in full sentences
- make a clear argument, not just a summary

## Submission
Submit at the end of class.

- clear use of course vocabulary
- quality of reasoning
- thoughtful discussion of evidence and ethics
- specificity about technique, workflow, and limitation
